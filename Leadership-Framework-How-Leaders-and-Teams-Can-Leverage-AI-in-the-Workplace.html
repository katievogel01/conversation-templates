How Leaders and Teams Can Leverage AI in the Workplace

AI isn't coming — it's already here, and the leaders who figure out how to use it well right now are going to be the ones setting the standard for everyone else. I've spent the last two years using AI every single day, building over 20 custom tools, and watching it completely change how I lead, communicate, and make decisions. This guide gives you the practical frameworks, use cases, and step-by-step process to start doing the same — no coding required, no tech background necessary.

The Biggest Mistakes People Make When Using AI

Avoid these four common pitfalls before going any further:

  1. Not training your AI. Using AI without teaching it about you, your work, and your goals produces generic, unusable output.
  2. Not using custom tools. Consistency requires using the same trained tool every time. You cannot scale results by starting from scratch in a new chat window each day.
  3. Blind trust. Copying AI output without careful review is one of the fastest ways to damage your credibility. AI is fast and helpful — and not ready to run the company.
  4. Not setting usage boundaries. Some tasks should not be done by AI. Define those boundaries for yourself and your team before problems arise.

The right mental model: AI is a junior intern. Fast, helpful, and not ready to run the company.

The Risks of AI in the Workplace

Understanding the risks makes you a more responsible and credible leader when AI conversations come up on your team or with your executives.

  • Over-dependency and degrading output. Skills and critical thinking dull when AI replaces too much of the actual work.
  • Elimination of entry-level roles. This creates an experience gap — the pipeline of future leaders thins when junior staff aren't doing the foundational work that builds expertise.
  • Less technically skilled staff. Reliance on AI without understanding the underlying task creates fragility. People who never learn the fundamentals can't catch AI errors.
  • Regulatory and organizational policy risk. Rules around AI use are evolving fast. Leaders who build AI-dependent workflows without accounting for compliance may face sudden disruption.
  • Reduced interpersonal connection. Replacing human-to-human communication with AI-generated content erodes team culture and trust over time.
  • Loss of creativity and inspiration. AI can make teams efficient but uninspired. Efficiency without vision is just faster mediocrity.

Understanding Human-AI Collaboration: The Hybrid Workflow Model

A hybrid workflow is a process where humans and AI work together — each handling the parts of a task they are best suited for — to achieve faster, more consistent, and higher-quality results.

AI does: repetitive tasks, data-heavy work, pattern recognition, summarizing, research, and organizing.

Humans do: judgment, emotional nuance, persuasion, ethics, creativity, final review, and discernment.

The goal is to assign the right work to the right "worker."

Example 1: Strategic Planning or Project Management

  1. Human brainstorms ideas, explores possibilities, and captures unfiltered thoughts.
  2. AI organizes and drafts the first version of the plan or framework.
  3. Human refines it with judgment, strategic context, and expanded insights.
  4. AI finalizes formatting, adds structure, or creates timelines and checklists.
  5. Human reviews, approves, and delivers the final strategy to the team.

Example 2: Communication and Team Alignment

  1. Human outlines main ideas, tone, or key points to communicate.
  2. AI drafts the first version of the message — email, meeting recap, or presentation outline.
  3. Human reviews for clarity, tone, and emotional nuance.
  4. AI formats, summarizes, or generates visuals such as slides, key bullets, or a summary email.
  5. Human delivers it with presence and persuasion.

Example 3: Data and Insights

  1. Human defines the question: "What story do we need the data to tell?"
  2. AI gathers and organizes data, runs initial summaries, or identifies patterns.
  3. Human interprets findings, adds context, and determines what actually matters.
  4. AI creates visualizations, dashboards, or summaries.
  5. Human makes strategic decisions and communicates the "why" to the team.

Example 4: Repetitive Task Automation and Elevation

  1. AI takes over the repetitive, time-consuming task — compiling reports, scheduling meetings, tracking progress, or generating routine summaries.
  2. Human reviews AI output for accuracy, adds insights, and identifies patterns or opportunities the system might miss.
  3. AI refines or updates based on feedback, ensuring consistency and speed going forward.
  4. Human uses the time saved to focus on higher-level work: improving the process, coaching the team, or making strategic decisions from the data.

How to Train an AI

Training AI means teaching it what you want it to know, sound like, and do. There are three ways to do this:

  • Prompts. You guide the AI's output by how clearly and specifically you ask. Better prompts produce dramatically better results.
  • Context. Provide a knowledge base that includes background information, samples, examples, and descriptions of ideal outputs.
  • Custom GPTs or fine-tuned tools. Build a dedicated tool trained specifically on your work, voice, and expertise — so it becomes an expert on you.

The goal: make the AI an expert on you and your work so that its outputs are immediately useful, not generic.

How to Build Your First Custom GPT Tool

Follow these seven steps to build a custom AI tool — no coding required:

  1. Open the GPT Builder. Navigate to the GPT Builder inside ChatGPT (available with a Plus or Team subscription).
  2. Plug in your business information. Prepare a Master Preset — a document that describes your role, voice, audience, goals, and communication style.
  3. Give it a name, description, and instructions. Paste your Master Preset into the instructions area so the tool knows exactly who it is working for and how to respond.
  4. Download your knowledge base documents as PDFs and upload them. This can include past work samples, SOPs, brand guidelines, or any reference material that helps the tool understand your context.
  5. Choose GPT features. Select which capabilities to enable, such as web browsing, image generation, or code execution, based on how you plan to use the tool.
  6. Create the GPT. Save the tool and give it a clear, memorable name that reflects its purpose.
  7. Test and refine. Run real prompts through it, review the outputs, and update the instructions or knowledge base as needed. Iteration is how you make it great.

Key Principles for Leaders Integrating AI

  • AI amplifies great leadership — it does not replace it. The better your judgment, expertise, and communication skills, the more powerful your AI output will be.
  • Set boundaries before you scale. Define which tasks AI can handle, which require human judgment, and what your team's usage guidelines are.
  • Keep humans in the loop. Every AI output should pass through a human review before it reaches a client, executive, or public audience.
  • Start simple, iterate fast. You do not need a perfect system to begin. Pick one repetitive task, experiment with AI on it, and build from there.
  • The leaders who learn this now will be the ones setting the standard later. Waiting for your company to train you is an option — but the leaders getting ahead are the ones who are not waiting.
