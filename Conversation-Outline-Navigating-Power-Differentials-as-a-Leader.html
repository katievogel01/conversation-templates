Navigating Power Differentials as a Leader

Most leadership conversations about power focus on what to do when you have less of it. This one flips the question: what kind of leader are you when you're the one holding it? The research is clear — even the best leadership strengths have a shadow side, and the leaders who do the most damage aren't malicious, they're unaware. This guide gives you the frameworks and language to stay calibrated, honest, and effective with the power you carry.

Types of Power You Hold as a Leader

Power differentials exist anytime there's a gap in authority, information, or access. They're not inherently bad — they're natural in leadership structures. But when unacknowledged, they create silence, self-protection, and disengagement.

  • Positional Power — authority from title or decision rights
  • Informational Power — control over resources or access
  • Emotional Power — how your tone, energy, and regulation shape the room
  • Relational Power — how belonging and inclusion flow through you
  • Cultural Power — norms you model that signal who "belongs"

Reflection: When people interact with you — what might feel safe, and what might feel intimidating? Remember: you decide how you show up, but they decide how you're experienced.

The Shadow Side of Your Strengths

Even the best intentions can go sideways under stress or ego. Here's how leadership strengths can morph into their shadows:

Leadership Strength Shadow Side When Unchecked
Confidence Arrogance, dismissal of others
Empathy Avoidance of hard feedback
Decisiveness Control, rigidity
Charisma Manipulation, over-influence
Vision Tunnel vision, neglect of input
Calm Emotional distance

Signs You Need to Recalibrate the Power Differential

A key skill to develop is the ability to sense when there is work to be done on your relationship with your team. Use this table to recognize the signals:

Signal What It Looks or Feels Like What It Might Mean
Silence or withdrawal Participants stop contributing, go quiet, speak only when called upon You may be unintentionally intimidating or shutting down psychological safety
Reluctance to disagree You don't hear objections or alternatives; few voice reservations Power distance is too large; people may be self-censoring
Delayed feedback / passive resistance After decisions are made, people drag their feet or subtly resist execution You might have bypassed input or been too directive
Overdependence — needing you for every decision Team members ask permission for small things, even when capable You may be hoarding authority rather than distributing it
Emotional signals — defensiveness, shutdown, overexertion You sense tension, stiffness, avoidance; or people becoming overly agreeable The interpersonal climate is unsafe or uneasy
Frequent misunderstandings or misalignment Outcomes differ from what was agreed; silent assumptions; miscommunication People were unclear or fearful to ask clarifying questions
Complaints about tone or delivery Someone raises that your delivery or energy felt sharp or distant Your warmth signal is being underheard, or competence is overshadowing connection
Lack of upward voice No one pushes back, questions, or suggests alternatives upward The power distance has grown so that "up" feels risky
Micromanagement creeping in You find yourself checking more, revising others' work, or reverting to "I'll just do it myself" You may have lost trust in others or feel pressure to control outcomes

Reflection: Which of these signals have you seen — maybe even subconsciously — but didn't name? What felt "off" in those moments?

The 5 Practices of Responsible Power

1. Transparency — Make decisions visible

  • "Here's what's behind this decision."
  • "Here's how I weighed the trade-offs."

2. Invitation — Encourage contribution, not compliance

  • "What perspectives haven't we heard yet?"
  • "What's one risk I might be missing here?"

3. Calibration — Check how your leadership lands

  • "How is this pace or style working for you?"
  • "Does this feel clear or rushed?"

4. Accountability — Own your influence

  • "I realize my tone may have just come across as sharp."
  • "I should've communicated earlier. I'll fix that."

5. Distributed Power — Give authority, not just tasks

  • "You own the final call on this — loop me in only if you hit a blocker."
  • "You have the trust and space to lead it your way."

Reflection: Identify one area where you could distribute power more effectively this week — and write an example of what that could look like.

Mindfulness Practice: Symbolic Intuition

This exercise invites you to notice what's being mirrored back to you in your current leadership dynamics.

  1. Sit comfortably. Close your eyes if you'd like.
  2. Bring to mind one recent leadership moment — a meeting, a tough conversation, or a quiet interaction that lingered with you. Don't try to control or choose the moment. Let your intuition pull forward what you need to see.
  3. Picture that moment like a still image. Notice where you are, what is happening, who is there with you. Enrich it with as much detail as you can.
  4. Ask yourself:
    • What quality of mine is reflected in this moment?
    • Am I seeing confidence, control, warmth, fear, or something else?
    • What might that moment have been trying to teach me?
  5. There's no need to judge it — just notice what stands out.
  6. Take a breath and repeat: Every interaction is feedback.
  7. When you're ready, open your eyes.

Integration Practice: The Power Audit Week

Put these concepts into motion with a focused week of observation:

  1. Choose one meeting or interaction this week where you normally hold most of the authority — a team update, a 1:1, or a project review.
  2. Set the intention beforehand: "My job is to notice power in motion."
  3. During or after, reflect on these three prompts:
    • Where did power flow easily through the room, and where did it bottleneck with me?
    • Did I sense any of the signals from the Power Imbalance table?
    • How could I redistribute ownership, decision-making, or credit next time?
  4. Capture one action step — even something small — to implement the following week.

Journal & Discussion Questions

  1. When I hold power, how do I want people to feel in my presence?
  2. Where might I unintentionally be creating distance, dependence, or silence?
  3. Which "shadow side" of my strengths have I seen peek through lately?
  4. What's one conversation or relationship that might need a power recalibration?
  5. How can I practice both warmth and competence more intentionally this week?
  6. What would it look like to trust others with power — even when it feels risky?
