10 Ways to Speak More Assertively In Reviews

Performance reviews are one of the highest-stakes conversations you'll have at work — and most people go in underprepared. Whether you're the one giving feedback or receiving it, how you communicate in that room shapes how you're perceived, what you get, and where you go next. Here's what to actually say.

1. Know Your Objective Before You Walk In

For leaders: Define the purpose of the review before the meeting — is it growth, alignment, recognition, or redirection? Your objective shapes your entire communication strategy.

For employees: Get clear on what you want out of this conversation. Is it a salary adjustment, role development, recognition, or clarity on your path forward? Know your goal so you can steer toward it.

2. Build Your Case with Data, Not Just Opinions

For leaders: Use specific metrics and examples to make feedback actionable rather than vague.

For employees: Come prepared with a list of accomplishments backed by numbers. If you haven't started a Wins Tracker yet, now is the time — document your impact as it happens so you're never scrambling before a review.

3. Plan Your Language and Energy in Advance

The words you choose signal confidence — or the absence of it. Avoid passive, hedging, or overly apologetic language. Practice assertive framing before the meeting:

  • "This quarter, I successfully completed X, which led to Y."
  • "I'd like to explore opportunities to contribute further in [specific area]."

4. Leaders: Start with Strengths

Opening with what's working isn't just nice — it builds the psychological safety that makes the rest of the conversation productive. Lead with genuine, specific recognition before moving to development areas.

Example: "You've shown consistent reliability in hitting project deadlines, which has been crucial for the team."

5. Leaders: Align Feedback to Their Goals

When your feedback connects to what an employee actually wants for their career, it lands differently. Start by asking, then tailor your message to their aspirations.

Questions to ask:

  • "What are your professional goals for the next six months to a year?"
  • "Where do you see yourself in three years, and how can we help you get there?"
  • "Are there any skills or experiences you'd like to focus on developing this year?"

Listen actively, take notes, and reflect their goals back to them in your feedback. Then reframe redirection with their aspirations in mind:

Instead of saying… Say…
"You need to improve your delegation skills." "Since your goal is to prepare for a leadership role, improving delegation will be key. Let's focus on strategies to empower your team while ensuring accountability."

6. Leaders: Use the "What, Why, How" Framework for Feedback

Clear, actionable feedback answers three questions every time:

  • What needs to improve?
  • Why is this important?
  • How can we address it together?

End the conversation with a concrete next step: "By the end of Q1, let's aim for [specific milestone]. Does that align with your goals?"

7. Employees: Lead with Your Wins

Don't wait to be asked — open with your impact. Frame your accomplishments around value delivered, not just tasks completed.

Example: "This project brought in a X% revenue increase, which aligns directly with our team's goals."

8. Employees: Get Ahead of Any Missed Goals

If something didn't go as planned, address it before your manager does. This shows self-awareness and maturity — two things that build trust with leadership. Cover three things:

  1. Acknowledge what happened
  2. Share what you learned
  3. Outline your next steps based on those learnings

Bonus points if you've already taken proactive steps before the review conversation.

9. Employees: Ask Assertive Questions

Asking sharp, intentional questions shifts you from passive recipient to active participant in your own career development. Use these by category:

Clarifying Expectations

  • "How do you define success in this role, and do you feel I'm meeting those expectations?"
  • "What are the top priorities you'd like me to focus on in the next quarter?"
  • "What does success look like to you in this area, and how can I ensure I'm meeting it?"

Strengths and Value

  • "What do you think are my greatest strengths, and how can I leverage them further?"
  • "What unique value do you think I bring to the team?"
  • "Are there areas where you feel I'm excelling that I should double down on?"

Career Development

  • "What do you see as my potential career path within this organization?"
  • "How can I prepare now for future opportunities in leadership or [specific role]?"
  • "What do I need to demonstrate to move to the next level?"

Compensation and Recognition

  • "Based on my performance this year, would you consider me eligible for a salary adjustment or bonus?"
  • "What milestones or metrics would I need to meet to be considered for a raise or promotion?"
  • "Can we discuss how my current responsibilities align with my compensation level?"

Growth Opportunities

  • "What opportunities do you see for me to expand my role?"
  • "Are there stretch assignments or leadership opportunities I should pursue?"
  • "Are there any upcoming projects where you think I could play a key role?"

Team and Company Alignment

  • "What are the company's top priorities for the next six months, and how can I align my work to support them?"
  • "What are your priorities for the team, and how can I align my goals with them?"
  • "Are there ways I can take more ownership of key initiatives?"

Addressing Challenges

  • "What are the biggest challenges you see for our team, and how can I help address them?"
  • "Are there any behaviors or habits you think I should adjust to improve my impact?"
  • "What potential obstacles should I be aware of in achieving my goals?"

Closing the Conversation

  • "How do you feel this discussion has gone?"
  • "Would it be helpful to schedule a follow-up to check in on progress?"
  • "What's one piece of advice you'd give me as I continue to grow in my role?"

10. Employees: Receive Feedback Without Defensiveness

How you respond to critical feedback in the moment matters as much as the feedback itself. Acknowledge it, ask a clarifying question, and reframe it as an opportunity rather than an attack.

Script: "I appreciate the insight. Could we discuss some specific ways I can address this area?"

After the review, create a written summary of what was discussed and agreed upon. Use it to track your own progress — and to hold your manager accountable to commitments made on their end too.
