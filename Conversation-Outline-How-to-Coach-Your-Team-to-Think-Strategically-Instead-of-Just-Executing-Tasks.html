Coaching Your Team to Become More Strategic Thinkers

One of the most common frustrations I hear from leaders is some version of "I wish my team would just think bigger." But here's the thing — strategic thinking isn't a personality trait, it's a skill, and your job as a leader is to coach people into it. This resource gives you the frameworks, questions, and language to do exactly that.

What Strategic Thinking Actually Means

Strategic thinking isn't abstract. It can be learned, strengthened, and coached. Before you can develop it in others, it helps to have a clear working definition.

Strategic thinkers consistently do five things:

  • Connect their daily work to the bigger picture
  • Anticipate risks, opportunities, and downstream impacts
  • Make intentional choices about where to focus their effort
  • Prioritize based on impact, not busyness
  • Offer solutions and options instead of waiting for direction

Working Definition: Strategic thinking is the ability to connect your work to the bigger picture, anticipate what might happen next, and make intentional choices that drive the greatest impact.

Why People Struggle to Think Strategically

When someone isn't thinking strategically, it usually comes down to one of four core barriers. Diagnosing the right barrier before you coach is what makes the difference between a conversation that lands and one that doesn't.

Barrier What It Looks Like Common Signs
Motivation / Buy-In They don't see the upside in going above and beyond, or don't understand how their work connects to broader strategy. "Just tell me what to do" · Lack of initiative · Minimal suggestions or ideas
Capacity / Time Strategic thinking requires focus. Many people are overwhelmed with tasks, firefighting, or constant context-switching. Always reacting, never planning · No deep work time · Stuck in the weeds
Capability / Understanding They haven't yet developed the business, industry, or role understanding needed to see the bigger picture. Strong executors but weak at prioritizing · Unclear on customers, goals, or KPIs · Difficulty connecting work to outcomes
Confidence / Psychological Safety They're afraid to be wrong, look naive, or offer a bad idea — so they default to task mode. Always waiting for direction · Rarely offering opinions or options · Keeping ideas to themselves

The 5-Step Coaching Framework for Building Strategic Thinkers

Strategic thinking grows with practice. The best way to build it is through structured coaching conversations that lift people from "task mode" to "strategy mode." Use this framework in 1:1s, project reviews, or any decision-making moment.

Step 1 — Explore the Situation

Goal: Understand how they currently see the issue.

  • "What's the current situation as you see it?"
  • "What's the main goal here?"
  • "Who else is impacted by this?"

Step 2 — Clarify the Core Challenge

Goal: Help them zoom out and see the bigger picture.

  • "What's the bigger goal behind this task?"
  • "What assumptions might we be making?"
  • "How does this align with our team or business goals?"

Step 3 — Generate Options

Goal: Encourage creative and proactive thinking.

  • "What are 2–3 different approaches we could take?"
  • "What's the simple option? What's the bold option?"
  • "If you had half the time or resources, what lever would you pull?"

Step 4 — Evaluate Trade-Offs & Decide

Goal: Strengthen judgment and prioritization.

  • "What are the trade-offs of each option?"
  • "Which option drives the greatest impact?"
  • "Where's the 80/20 — what small effort produces the biggest result?"

Step 5 — Reflect & Reinforce the Muscle

Goal: Turn strategic thinking into a habit.

  • "What did you learn from how you approached this?"
  • "What would you do differently next time?"
  • "Where did you feel confident vs. stuck?"

Putting It Into Practice

You don't need to overhaul your 1:1s to start using this. Here's how to work it in right away:

  • Bring this structure into your next 1:1 when a team member brings you a problem or decision
  • Use the questions to guide project decisions instead of giving answers directly
  • Share the framework with your team so everyone speaks the same "strategy language"
  • Build regular pauses into your workflow for thinking, not just doing

Strategic thinking strengthens when managers consistently ask better questions, provide clarity, and encourage people to connect their work to the larger goals. Your questions are the lever.

Three Ideas to Carry Forward

  1. Strategic thinking is coached — not assigned. Telling someone "be more strategic" is vague. Showing them how is transformational.
  2. Remove the barriers first. People need context, time, and psychological safety to think at a higher level. If those aren't in place, no coaching framework will stick.
  3. Model it openly. Share how you evaluate trade-offs, choose priorities, and make decisions. When you think out loud, your team learns by osmosis.

If you want more strategic thinkers on your team, create the environment where strategic thinking is possible — and coach people into it with intentional questions.
