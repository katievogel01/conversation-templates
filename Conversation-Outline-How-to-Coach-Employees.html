How to Coach Employees

The best leaders I know aren't the ones with all the answers — they're the ones who know how to ask the right questions. Coaching your team isn't about fixing problems for people; it's about helping them build the confidence and capability to solve problems themselves. These tools will help you make that shift in a way that actually sticks.

Mindset Shift: From Directive to Coach

Before diving into the skills, the most important move is internal. Coaching requires a different posture than managing.

  • Move from "Do this…" to "What do you think would work best?"
  • Your job isn't to have all the answers — it's to help your employee find theirs.
  • The best coaches focus on empowering, not solving.

Reflection prompt: Think of a time someone guided you instead of telling you what to do. How did it change your perspective?

Mindfulness Minute: Getting into a Coaching State

Before a coaching conversation, use this 90-second reset to shift from problem-solving mode into a calm, present, and curious mindset.

Step 1 — Set the Stage (10 seconds)

Say to yourself: "Coaching is about presence, curiosity, and deep listening." Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Take one deep breath in and slowly exhale.

Step 2 — Grounding Breathwork (30 seconds)

Breathe in slowly through your nose… hold… exhale slowly. Repeat three times. As you inhale, create space for curiosity. As you exhale, let go of the need to fix or solve.

Step 3 — Sensory Awareness (20 seconds)

Notice your feet on the ground, the weight of your body in your seat, the rhythm of your breath. Feel yourself fully here, fully present.

Step 4 — Intention Setting (20–30 seconds)

Bring to mind one word that represents the kind of leader you want to be right now — maybe it's listener, guide, or mentor. Hold that word as you start the conversation.

Step 5 — Gentle Transition (10 seconds)

Take one last deep breath and slowly open your eyes.

Core Coaching Skill 1: Active Listening & Presence

Key insight: Employees won't open up if they don't feel heard.

  • Stop multitasking — your presence is your power.
  • Use reflective listening: repeat or paraphrase what they say.
  • Silence is powerful — give them space to think before you jump in.

Try This

When an employee shares a challenge, respond with one of these before offering any advice:

  • "So what I'm hearing is…"
  • "It sounds like the biggest challenge is…"

Core Coaching Skill 2: Asking Powerful Questions

Key insight: The best leaders ask rather than tell.

Instead of this… Try this…
"Here's what you should do." "What solutions have you considered?"
"That won't work." "What obstacles do you see, and how might you work around them?"
"Here's what you should do." "What options have you considered?"

Go-To Coaching Questions

  1. "What's the real challenge here?"
  2. "What's one action you can take this week?"
  3. "What's stopping you from moving forward?"

Try This

In your next 1:1, ask only questions for the first 5 minutes before offering any advice.

Core Coaching Skill 3: Accountability Without Micromanaging

Key insight: Coaching means guiding action, not taking over.

The What / When / How Framework

When an employee commits to something, confirm it with three questions:

  1. What will you do?
  2. When will you do it?
  3. How will I know?

Example:
Employee: "I'll finish the project soon."
Coach: "When exactly? How will I know it's done?"

Try This

Next time an employee commits to something, follow up with: "By when?" and "How will I know?"

Core Coaching Skill 4: Feedback That Fuels Growth

Key insight: Good feedback challenges people while building trust — not tearing it down.

The 5A Feedback Model

  1. Ask for Permission — "Can I share an observation?"
  2. Acknowledge Strengths — "I appreciate how you handled…"
  3. Actionable Feedback — "Here's what could be even better…"
  4. Align with Goals — "This will help you get to…"
  5. Ask for Their Perspective — "What do you think?"

Example:
Instead of: "You need to communicate better in meetings."
Try: "I noticed in the last meeting your idea was strong, but it got overlooked. What would help you speak up more next time?"

Try This

Use the 5A model in your next feedback conversation — even just the first two steps make a noticeable difference.

Core Coaching Skill 5: Stretch + Support — Pushing Employees to Grow

Key insight: Great coaches push people beyond self-imposed limits — and then stand behind them.

The Stretch + Support Formula

  1. Stretch: Challenge them beyond their comfort zone.
  2. Support: Offer guidance, not micromanagement.

Example:
Employee: "I think I can make 3 client calls this week."
Coach: "How about 5? What would it take to make that happen?"

Try This

In your next 1:1, challenge an employee to go one step further than they initially suggest. Then ask what support they need to get there.

Key Takeaways

  1. Listen more than you speak.
  2. Ask powerful questions instead of giving advice.
  3. Hold employees accountable with the What / When / How framework.
  4. Give feedback that's both direct and encouraging — use the 5A model.
  5. Challenge employees to stretch, then support them in getting there.

Your Action Plan

  • Today: Use one coaching question in a conversation.
  • This week: Challenge one employee to go further than they suggest, then ask what support they need.
  • Ongoing: Shift from "telling" to "coaching" — aim for a 70/30 ratio (you listen 70% of the time).

The 1:1 Coaching Presence Challenge

Your next 1:1 is the perfect place to practice everything above. Leaders who embrace a coaching presence don't just manage — they develop and empower their teams. Here's exactly how to do it.

Before the Meeting

  • Take one minute to center yourself with a deep breath.
  • Set an intention: How do I want to show up as a coach?
  • Remind yourself: My role is to listen, ask, and empower — not to fix.

During the Meeting

  • Aim for a 70/30 ratio — they talk, you listen.
  • Hold space for silence — give them time to think before you jump in.
  • Replace advice with coaching questions:
    Instead of "Here's what you should do," try "What options have you considered?"
    Instead of "That won't work," try "What obstacles do you see, and how might you work around them?"
