How to Motivate Employees

Motivation isn't about pushing people harder — it's about pulling out what already matters to them and creating the conditions for that drive to thrive. The leaders who get this right stop trying to manufacture enthusiasm and start getting genuinely curious about each person on their team. And here's the thing nobody says enough: your energy is the starting point. If you show up flat, your team will too.

The Core Principle: Motivation Is Personal

What motivates you may not motivate them. Your job as a leader is to uncover what already matters to each person — and connect that to the work they're doing every day.

Start with this question: "When have you felt truly motivated at work? What made the difference?"

8 Ways to Unlock Motivation on Your Team

1. Give Them Something to Look Forward To

Momentum matters. Create energy around milestones, growth, and even small wins. People stay engaged when there's something on the horizon worth working toward.

  • Upcoming launches or product releases
  • Promotions or stretch assignments
  • Team events or new projects
  • The chance to build a skill they've been wanting to develop

2. Understand Their Goals — Or Help Them Set Some

Many employees are never asked what they actually want. Motivation increases when work aligns with a personal sense of progress and achievement.

Ask:

  • "What would feel amazing to accomplish this month?"
  • "What skills or opportunities are you hungry for?"

3. Establish Team Goals and Check In Often

Collective goals keep people rowing in the same direction. Track them visibly, talk about them consistently, and tie daily work back to the bigger picture.

Pro tip: Open weekly meetings with — "What's one win this week that moved us toward our goal?" It takes two minutes and keeps the mission front of mind.

4. Celebrate Progress — Together

Whether one person hit a milestone or the whole team crossed a finish line, celebrate it as a team. Shared recognition builds camaraderie, appreciation, and collective investment in what you're building.

Ideas to try:

  • Team shout-outs in meetings or group channels
  • Mark the end of a sprint or project with a real acknowledgment
  • Rotate who gets to "ring the bell" when something great happens

5. Know the Difference Between Motivation and Accountability

These two things work together — but they're not the same.

Motivation Accountability
Internal drive — answers why someone cares External structure — ensures follow-through
Comes from values, goals, and meaning Comes from clear expectations and check-ins

When both are present, people feel excited to show up and supported to deliver.

Example script: "You've said this project is really important to your growth — let's put a plan in place to make sure you get what you need to hit that milestone."

6. Create a Culture of Appreciation and Respect

Motivation increases when people feel seen for who they are, valued for what they contribute, and respected for their effort, input, and individuality. This isn't about big gestures — it's about consistency.

Simple scripts that land:

  • "I noticed how you handled that — impressive work."
  • "Your effort on this project didn't go unnoticed."

7. Use the Autonomy, Mastery, and Purpose Framework

Dan Pink's model holds up because it maps to how humans are actually wired. Use it as a diagnostic when someone seems disengaged.

Driver What It Means Question to Ask
Autonomy Control over how they get things done "Where do you want more ownership?"
Mastery The chance to grow and build skills they care about "What's a skill you'd love to develop?"
Purpose Connection to a bigger story — impact, mission, team success "How does this work connect to something meaningful for you?"

8. Own Your Energy — It's Contagious

Your team mirrors you. Flat energy, burned-out energy, and checked-out energy all trickle down fast. You don't have to be fake or perform enthusiasm you don't feel — but you do need to lead with clarity, conviction, and care.

Check yourself:

  • "What energy am I bringing into the room?"
  • "Do I visibly care about what we're working on?"
  • "Am I showing them that I believe in where we're going?"

Practical resets:

  • Share something you're genuinely excited about at the start of the week
  • Let your passion show when talking about the mission — don't bury it in logistics
  • Name what's going well before diving into what needs fixing
