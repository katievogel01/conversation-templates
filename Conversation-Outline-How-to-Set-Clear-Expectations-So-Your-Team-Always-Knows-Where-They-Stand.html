Expectations of Leadership

One of the questions I hear most from women stepping into leadership is some version of: "Am I doing this right?" Here's what I want you to know — leading well isn't about having all the answers or doing everything perfectly. It's about being clear, being intentional, and showing up for your people in the ways that actually move things forward.

The Real Job of a Leader

Leadership is not expertise — it's decision-making. Your job is not to be the smartest person in the room or the one who does everything. Your job is to set direction, create clarity, develop your people, and hold the standard. That's it.

Common Myths That Keep Women Leaders Stuck

Myth Reality
"I should have all the answers." You're allowed to say "I'll get back to you." Leadership isn't expertise — it's decision-making.
"It's faster if I do it myself." True in the short term. But long term? You'll burn out and cap your team's growth.
"I don't want to come off bossy." Direct = clear. Clarity is kind. Being respected requires being clear.
"I can't delegate until I've perfected it myself." Leadership is not about perfection — it's about trust and transfer.

The 5 Core Responsibilities of a Leader

1. Set the Vision

Your job is to see the bigger picture — to zoom out while others are zoomed in. That means:

  • Clarifying what success looks like for your team.
  • Making sure everyone knows the "why" behind the work.
  • Saying "This is where we're going. Here's why it matters."

Ask yourself: Have I clearly communicated what great looks like?

2. Create Structure and Set Expectations

People don't resist hard work — they resist confusion. Your job is to make things clear.

  • Define roles, responsibilities, and priorities.
  • Set standards for communication, ownership, timelines, and accountability.
  • Establish how your team will work together, solve problems, and make decisions.

Ask yourself: Is everyone on my team clear on what's expected — and what's not?

3. Coach, Don't Just Do

This is where so many talented women get stuck. You were great at your old job — now your job is to make others great.

  • Stop jumping in to fix.
  • Start asking: "What's your recommendation?" "What do you need from me?" "What's your plan?"
  • You're no longer the expert doing the work — you're the expert building the people who do the work.

Remember: "If I always step in, they'll never step up."

4. Make Strategic Decisions

Strategy isn't just about long-term planning. It's about choosing what matters most right now.

  • Where will we focus time, energy, and resources?
  • What can we say no to?
  • How do we protect the team from distraction?

Remember: If you're doing everything, you're doing strategy wrong.

5. Model Emotional Leadership

You're not just managing tasks — you're managing energy.

  • Stay steady in the chaos. Be a thermostat, not a thermometer.
  • Regulate your tone, pace, and presence.
  • Set a tone of clarity, calm, and confidence — even when things are messy.

Ask yourself: Am I creating the energy I want my team to mirror?

Leadership Weekly Habits

These five habits, done consistently every week, will make you a leader your team trusts and your organization values.

  1. Set (or reset) team priorities.
  2. Ask: "Where are we stuck?" and unblock progress.
  3. Recognize wins. People perform better when they're seen.
  4. Delegate something that's not the best use of your time.
  5. Reflect: "Did I lead — or did I just get stuff done?"

Coaching Questions to Come Back To

Use these as a weekly self-check or as conversation starters with your team:

  • Have I clearly communicated what great looks like?
  • Is everyone on my team clear on what's expected — and what's not?
  • Am I coaching, or am I just doing?
  • What am I holding onto that I should be handing off?
  • Am I creating the energy I want my team to mirror?
  • Did I lead this week — or did I just get stuff done?
