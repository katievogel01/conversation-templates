High-Impact 1:1 Meetings — Conversation Template

Category: Performance Management & Team Development

Best used when: You're leading recurring 1:1s with direct reports and want a structured, repeatable system — whether you're resetting the quality of existing meetings or establishing healthy habits with a new team member.

Most 1:1 meetings drain everyone in the room because no one was ever given a real system for running them. When you walk in with a clear structure, you stop reacting and start actually leading — and your people feel the difference immediately. This template gives you the exact framework and language to make every 1:1 productive, trust-building, and worth everyone's time.

The Framework: The 5-Step 1:1 Structure

Think of this as your "agenda backbone" — follow it in every meeting.

Step 1 — Human Check-In (2–5 minutes)

Build connection and context without losing control of the meeting. Keep this intentional and time-bound — you are creating safety, not opening the floor indefinitely.

  • "What's new in your world? Any exciting updates?"
  • "What's been going really well for you recently? Work or not."
  • "What's taking up the most brain space this week?"

Step 2 — Wins, Losses, and What's Next (5–10 minutes)

Reinforce progress, normalize learning, and build confidence. This is how you create a team that is willing to take smart risks instead of playing small.

  • "What's a win worth celebrating?"
  • "What didn't go as planned — and what did you learn?"
  • "What are you trying next?"

Step 3 — Priorities and Blockers (10 minutes)

Ensure alignment and remove friction. If you skip this step, you will spend your week reacting instead of leading.

  • "What are your top 1–3 priorities right now?"
  • "What's slowing you down?"
  • "Where do you need support from me?"

Step 4 — Coaching and Development (10–15 minutes)

Develop your people and improve performance. This is the most commonly skipped — and most important — part of the meeting. Do not wait until this moment to give corrective feedback you've put off for two weeks. Use this time for growth-minded coaching, not delayed discipline.

  • "I've noticed a pattern I want to talk through…"
  • "One area I think you're ready to level up in is…"
  • "What feedback would be most useful right now?"

Step 5 — Next Steps and Close (3–5 minutes)

Create clarity and accountability. Always leave with clear decisions, defined priorities, assigned ownership, and known follow-ups. If it is not written down or agreed upon clearly, it will not happen.

Setting Expectations With Your Team

Your team will only show up well to 1:1s if you teach them how. Set the standard clearly from the start.

What to Say When You Set the Standard

  • "Our 1:1s are your time. Each week, come prepared with: your Wins, Losses, What's Next; where you're blocked; and what you want to get my perspective on."

Non-Opener / Accountability Standard

  • Use a shared Google Doc or Notion doc — both parties contribute before the meeting.
  • If there is no preparation — meaning they have not put anything in the prep doc — the meeting gets rescheduled.

Shifting Ownership of Development

  • Help them understand: this is their growth time.
  • You are there to guide, not carry.

When It Gets Hard

Situation What's Happening What to Say
The Chronic Venter The meeting becomes an emotional download with no resolution. "I can tell this is weighing on you. What support would be most helpful — clarity, a decision, or a plan?"
The Underprepared Employee They show up with nothing to discuss, or launch into a recap of the past two weeks. "I want to make sure this time is valuable. Next week, come with three bullets: wins/losses, blockers, and where you want support. If that's missing, we'll reschedule."
The Defensive Employee They resist or deflect feedback. "I'm sharing this because I believe in your ability to grow through it and because it's an expectation."
The High Performer Burning Out Strong results, but unsustainable pace. "What feels unsustainable right now?" / "What can we remove or adjust?"

Closing the Conversation

After each 1:1, send a short recap. Keep it simple — this should take 2–3 minutes. Use bullet lists and brevity.

  • Decisions made
  • Priorities for the week
  • Blockers and actions
  • Follow-ups

Key principle: Clarity after the meeting is just as important as the meeting itself.
