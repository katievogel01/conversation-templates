How to Identify Top Talent and Make It Visible

One of the most important things you can do as a leader is see people before they fully see themselves — and then make sure the right people see them too. This guide walks you through how to spot high-potential talent on your team, how to invest in their growth, and how to advocate for them strategically, even when others aren't on board yet.

1. Identifying High-Potential Talent

What to Look For

These five traits are the early indicators that someone has leadership potential — watch for them in everyday moments, not just formal reviews.

Trait What It Looks Like in Practice
Initiative They take ownership and solve problems without being asked.
Adaptability They respond to change and setbacks with resilience, not resistance.
Influence Peers naturally seek their input or follow their lead — even without a title.
Growth Mindset They are coachable, curious, and actively eager to learn.
Integrity & Accountability They admit mistakes, own the outcome, and course-correct without being pushed.

How to Nurture It

Once you've spotted potential, intentional investment is what turns it into growth.

  • Assign stretch assignments that reveal capability under real pressure.
  • Use coaching-style feedback — future-focused questions, not just corrections.
  • Invite them to shadow leadership meetings so they see how decisions get made.
  • Give them a small project or cross-functional initiative to lead from start to finish.
  • Hold regular 1:1s focused on career goals and development — not just task updates.

2. Making Their Value Visible to Others

How to Advocate Upward

Your words carry weight in rooms they're not in yet. Use them.

  • Lead with specific examples of impact, not general praise.
    Example: "She identified a systems issue that saved the team 14 hours a week."
  • Use language that signals readiness and trust:
    "I'd trust him to run this if I stepped away — he's been operating like a team lead already."
    "She's not loud in meetings, but she quietly pulls people together and leads from the middle."
  • Bring their name up strategically in conversations with senior leadership — not as a favor, as a fact.

How to Create Visibility

  • Give them credit by name in team and executive meetings: "That was all Maria — she led that initiative."
  • Invite them to present project updates or recaps directly to stakeholders.
  • Nominate them for internal committees, cross-functional projects, or task forces that put them in front of new audiences.

3. When Others Don't See Their Value Yet

This situation is common and politically delicate. Here's how to handle it with strategy and patience.

Tactics That Work

  • Teach them to master the Professional Brag — and start bragging for them yourself. Keep it subtle and specific, not promotional.
  • Build evidence over time: track wins, growth metrics, and positive feedback from others so you have data, not just opinion.
  • Involve others in their mentorship or cross-functional projects — broader exposure creates broader buy-in.
  • If someone is being overlooked because of bias, name it directly:
    "I wonder if we're undervaluing her contribution because she doesn't speak in the same style."
  • Use open-ended questions to reframe perceptions:
    "What would you need to see to view him as ready for the next level?"

What Not to Do

  • Don't argue or try to force their promotion — it tends to backfire.
  • Don't stop mentoring them in the meantime. Keep investing and keep building broader support quietly.
