The Strategic Ask — Conversation Template

Category: Strategic Communication & Self-Advocacy

Best used when: You need to make a clear request — for resources, support, career advancement, buy-in, or a decision — and want to communicate it in a way that positions you as credible, composed, and collaborative rather than demanding or apologetic.

Most leaders know what they need. The part that trips them up is how to say it out loud without over-explaining, under-selling, or bracing for a fight. A strategic ask isn't about being aggressive — it's about being clear. When you know what you want, connect it to something bigger than yourself, and deliver it with calm authority, you shift from hoping someone notices you to leading the conversation. That's the work here.

The Framework

A strategic ask is clear, considered, and connected to shared goals. Three elements make it land:

1. Clarity — Know What You're Asking For

Most people talk around what they want instead of naming it. How you start determines how you'll be heard.

  • "What I need…"
  • "What I'm asking…"
  • "What I'm proposing…"
  • "To move this forward, I need…"
  • "I've given this some thought, and I'd like to request…"
  • "I'd like to have a direct conversation about…"
  • "It's important to me that I communicate this clearly…"
  • "In order to do my best work, I need…"
  • "I'd like to get your perspective on…"

2. Strategy — Frame It Through Shared Wins

Every ask needs a "why" that connects beyond yourself.

  • "This approach would free our team up to focus on ___, which supports the department's goal of ___."
  • "By investing in this now, we can prevent ___ down the line."
  • "I'd like approval to pilot this with one client — it'll help us validate before scaling."
  • "I'd like to take the lead on this initiative — it's directly tied to the skills you said are key for the next level."
  • "Can we align on expectations so we're not duplicating work? It'll make both our teams more effective."

3. Confidence — Deliver It with Calm Authority

Confidence doesn't mean pushy. It means steady, unapologetic energy.

Practice Tip: When you feel yourself rushing or over-explaining — pause, breathe, and hold eye contact for two seconds. That pause is power.

Reframe: "A confident ask isn't about proving your worth. It's about assuming your worth and negotiating from it."

Quick-Reference Scripts

Business / Project-Related Asks

These establish clarity, initiative, and alignment with company goals.

For resources or approval

  • "To move this project forward, what I need is approval for ___."
  • "I'd like to request additional support in ___ so we can hit our timeline."
  • "I'm asking for budget to test this approach — it directly supports our target of ___."
  • "Here's what I'm proposing and what I'll need from leadership to execute."

For decision-making clarity

  • "Can we make a clear decision today on ___ so my team can move forward?"
  • "I want to confirm who's making the final call on ___ to avoid duplication or delays."

For process improvements

  • "I'd like to revisit how we're handling ___ because there's an opportunity to improve efficiency and outcomes."
  • "I'm asking for a short trial period to test ___ — we'll evaluate results after two weeks."

Career Advancement Asks

These communicate readiness, initiative, and ownership without defensiveness.

For new responsibilities or leadership opportunities

  • "I'd like to take the lead on ___ — it's directly aligned with my development goals and the company's priorities."
  • "I'm interested in stepping into a larger leadership role on this initiative — can we discuss what that path could look like?"
  • "I'm ready to take on more ownership in ___; what would it take to make that happen?"

For raises or promotions

  • "I'd like to discuss aligning my compensation with the level of responsibility I've been leading at."
  • "I'd like to have a conversation about next steps toward a promotion to ___."
  • "I'd like to review my growth over the last 12 months and discuss how that aligns with advancement opportunities."

For mentorship or visibility

  • "I'd love to explore ways to gain more visibility with senior leadership — would you be open to discussing how I can do that strategically?"
  • "Would you be willing to mentor me around ___ as I step into this next phase of leadership?"

Collaboration / Support Asks

These model healthy assertiveness and emotional intelligence when leading across peers or teams.

For alignment or shared ownership

  • "Can we clarify who owns which parts of this project so expectations are clear for everyone?"
  • "I want to make sure we're aligned before we move forward — can we talk through how we'll handle ___?"

For boundary-setting

  • "I want to support this project, but to do it effectively I'll need to prioritize ___ and delegate ___."
  • "To meet this deadline, I'll need clearer parameters on what's expected and by when."

For accountability or feedback

  • "Can we check in on ___ to make sure we're on the same page about next steps?"
  • "I'd appreciate more clarity on how you'd like updates shared — what's most helpful for you?"

Leadership + Culture Asks

These are ideal for women challenging old dynamics or leading cultural change.

For inclusion or respect

  • "I'd like to revisit how feedback is shared in meetings to ensure everyone's voice is heard."
  • "I want to create more space for collaboration — can we discuss how to make that a team norm?"
  • "I'd like to talk about how we can make this environment more supportive for all levels of leadership."

For self-advocacy

  • "I'd like to share an idea I believe will improve both performance and morale."
  • "I want to talk about the impact this has had on my ability to lead effectively and propose a solution."

When It Gets Hard

When someone dismisses, deflects, or tries to flip the power dynamic — stay factual and future-focused. You don't match the tone; you stay neutral. That calm response subtly communicates: I'm not here to prove my worth to you; I'm here to lead with it.

Behavior How It Sounds Strategic Response
Deflection "You're being too sensitive." "I'm not questioning intent — I'm clarifying impact. Let's come back to the request."
Minimizing "That's not really a big deal." "It may seem small, but it directly affects ___ and the team's ability to ___."
Dismissal "We've already tried that." "That's fair — can we look at what's changed since last time?"
Gaslighting "You're imagining things." "I'm confident in what I observed. Let's focus on solving it together."
Judgment disguised as curiosity "Why do you want that?" (tone implying you're greedy, needy, or overstepping) "Because it will allow me to contribute at a higher level." — OR — "Because it's the next logical step based on the goals we've set." — OR — "Because it's aligned with how we've defined success for this role/project."

Assertive Techniques for Holding Ground

  1. Anchor back to facts. "Here's what I said. Here's what happened. Here's what I'm asking."
  2. Broken Record. Calmly restate your core point without defensiveness. "As I mentioned, what I'm asking for is ___ because it moves us toward ___."
  3. Regulate before reacting. Slow your breath. Drop your shoulders. Lower your tone. Your nervous system leads the room — not the other way around.
  4. Never debate. If you start defending or debating your perspective, you've already lost the power dynamic. Instead of arguing why you're right, redirect back to shared goals: "It sounds like we see this differently, but here's what we're both solving for…"
  5. Set boundaries if needed.
    • "I'm open to discussion, but not to being spoken to that way."
    • "Let's continue this when we can have a productive tone."
