How to Define Your Core Values as a Leader

Your values aren't a poster on a wall — they're the reason your team trusts you, the filter you use when decisions get hard, and the thing that makes your leadership style yours. I've watched women completely transform how they show up at work once they get clear on this, and today we're doing the work to get you there.

Mindfulness Minute

Before we dive in, take 60 seconds to settle in. This isn't fluff — grounding yourself before reflective work makes a real difference in what you're able to access.

Posture (10 seconds)

Place both feet flat on the floor. Sit tall but relaxed, with your shoulders soft. Imagine a string gently pulling you upward from the crown of your head.

Breath (20 seconds)

Take a slow, deep breath in through your nose… hold for a count of two… and release through your mouth. Let's do two more like that — breathing in… holding… and slowly releasing. Feel your body settling with each exhale.

Body Awareness (20 seconds)

Now bring your attention to your chest and heart area. Imagine this space expanding as you breathe in. With each exhale, picture releasing tension, control, or stress. With each inhale, invite clarity, openness, and presence.

Leadership Intention (10 seconds)

Silently ask yourself: How do I want to show up as a leader today? Notice the first word or image that comes to mind — hold onto it as your anchor for this training.

Why Core Values Matter in Leadership

As leaders, you're not just making decisions for yourself — you're shaping culture, trust, and results for everyone around you. Your core values are your North Star. They help you:

  • Make consistent decisions.
  • Build credibility with your team.
  • Create a leadership style that feels authentic.

Without them, leadership can feel reactive or scattered. With them, you get clarity and consistency.

How I do this: My #1 personal core value is FREEDOM. That shows up in my leadership in a lot of ways, but one big one is that I never micromanage people. I believe in giving others the freedom to own their roles and bring their creativity. Yes, I like processes and structure — but I always stay open to new ideas and give space for people to lead in their way. In fact, when I lay out key milestones for new hires I specifically tell them that by month six I'm looking for them to start "owning their role" — freely making process changes and optimizing the way they get the work done.

10 Steps to Defining Your Leadership Core Values

Step 1: Reflect on Past Leadership Experiences

  • Think of the times you were most proud as a leader.
  • What values were you honoring? (Trust, courage, growth, accountability?)

Step 2: Identify Your "Leadership Origin Values"

  • Who influenced your leadership style — bosses, mentors, teachers?
  • Which values do you want to emulate? Which do you reject?

Step 3: Review the Leadership Values List

  • Use the Leadership Values Word List below.
  • Highlight 10–15 words that resonate with how you want to show up as a leader.

Step 4: Write Freely

  • Free-write for 15 minutes about why those words matter.
  • Prompts: What does this value look like in action? Why does it matter to my leadership?

Step 5: Narrow Down

  • Group similar words together.
  • Cut down to 4–5 values.

Step 6: Define Each in Your Own Words

  • Example: "Accountability means I own my mistakes publicly and set the same expectation for my team."

Step 7: Prioritize Your Top 3–5

  • Ask: If my team only knew me for these 3–5 values, would I feel proud?

Step 8: Reflection and Alignment

  • Where does your current leadership align with these values? Where are you off?
  • Example: If "Respect" is a value but you often interrupt people, you've found a gap to work on.

Step 9: Test in Context

  • Take a real leadership challenge you're facing.
  • Ask: If I applied these values, what decision would I make?

Step 10: Live and Communicate Your Values

  • Model them consistently in your daily actions.
  • Talk about them openly with your team.
  • Recognize others when they reflect those values in action.
